# Tournament domain

The domain model is open for discussion. The examples below describe the intended event, not fixed rules or a final data model.

- Multiple stations host games and collect results such as points or times.
- An initial round combines results from two stations into an overall table.
- Later stages may include a four-player simultaneous semifinal and a final with several rounds in a fighting game.
- Game-selection wheels and a tournament tree should be available for presentation.
- The format should be configurable enough to support this event and future tournaments.

## Decisions

For now, each tournament participant represents one person. Teams may be supported in the future.

A participant has a display name, which may be a nickname. Their real name can also be stored as background data, but is optional: the display name may be their real name or otherwise sufficient to identify them. Where the real name is visible remains to be decided.

The organizer can set up the tournament structure and participants in advance. Participant details and the participant list must be quick to edit to accommodate late additions, withdrawals, and no-shows. A no-show can be removed from the participant list before play starts. If a participant is removed after play has begun, they are treated as forfeited throughout the tournament, and their existing results remain in the record. If their removal eliminates them from the active stage, replacement depends on the stage format. A scoring stage such as the initial stage can accept a player; an already active simultaneous semifinal or final cannot add a replacement. Where replacement is possible, bring in the next eligible player from the previous stage. If no eligible replacement is available, the organizer needs to handle the resulting shortfall. Withdrawal applies a stage-wide forfeit, resolving their remaining station games so the stage can be closed. Withdrawn participants remain visible in the leaderboard and are marked as withdrawn. They are excluded from advancement: eligible participants ranked below them move up when advancement places are assigned. The organizer can reactivate a withdrawn participant before the next stage begins, retaining their existing results. The effect of later reactivation remains to be decided.

A tournament has an ordered sequence of configurable stages. The organizer builds its structure by selecting stages of different kinds and configuring their details. The number advancing from a stage must match the configured participant count of the follow-up stage; the next stage therefore determines how many places are available. Qualifiers are placed automatically into the next stage once advancement is resolved, including any required tie-breaker. If too few eligible participants remain to fill the next stage, Retromania warns the organizer, who can adjust the tournament structure on the fly. A game may support multiple player counts; for example, the currently contemplated semifinal game can also be played with three players. Stage settings, including participant count, can be edited during the tournament only if the stage has no results yet, such as changing a semifinal from four players to three when its game supports both. A vacant slot is distinct from a participant and may be useful in formats with fixed positions, such as an elimination bracket; it has no scores and cannot advance. Detailed adjustment and bye rules remain to be decided. A tie-breaker stage can be configured in advance and is played only when a tie affects an advancement spot. It can remain hidden until needed. Only the tied participants competing for an advancement spot take part. Place it between the stage whose cutoff may be tied and the next regular stage. Players above the tie advance automatically; the tie-breaker order fills the remaining places. While its outcome is in use, reopen the preceding stage before changing the next stage’s player count.

A scoring stage combines results from its configured stations into one leaderboard. Each station uses either one fixed game or a pool, from which a game is randomly selected using its own wheel. An administrator can quickly edit the game pool for a round until its game is fixed, without recreating the tournament. The wheel selection becomes fixed only when someone confirms it. Anyone can spin, reject the selection, spin again, and confirm without administrator access. After confirmation, an administrator can unlock and change it if no results have been entered for that game. Games on a wheel have equal chances. The same game can appear at multiple stations; the organizer may confirm a repeated game or spin again. A separate reusable game library and game information pages are not needed for now. Every participant is expected to play every station; not playing a game is a forfeit. The organizer explicitly records a forfeit for the whole stage; a missing result remains pending rather than automatically becoming a forfeit. A stage-wide forfeit applies to all games in the stage and can be reversed for all of them until the stage is closed. The stage is ready to close only when every participant has a result for every required attempt at each station or a recorded stage-wide forfeit, preventing accidental closure with unresolved results. Anyone can close each station on its station page once its required results are resolved. When all stations are closed, the stage automatically attempts to close and advance the eligible players. If advancement is blocked, the stations remain closed and the reason is shown. If a player stops early, a dummy value can be entered for an unused attempt. A result already recorded before the stage-wide forfeit is retained for the record and possible reactivation, but does not earn leaderboard points while the forfeit is active. Unplayed, forfeited games also award zero leaderboard points. The participant appears below all active participants on the combined stage leaderboard and is marked as forfeited or withdrawn. A participant who forfeits the stage is ineligible to advance from it and is excluded from each game ranking when ranking points are awarded; remaining players move up. Saved results remain available for the record and possible reactivation. Reversing the forfeit restores saved results to the game rankings and recalculates ranking points and the combined leaderboard. Players can select a participant from the table for an attempt without a result and submit it without an administrator login, including from a shared tablet; no administrator confirmation is required. The app must remain available for spectators to check rankings while an active player plays. New eliminations, tie-breaker outcomes, and fighting-round wins can likewise be entered without a PIN; editing entered results requires an administrator. Editing or deleting an already entered result requires an administrator, who can correct mistakes or delete an attempt to allow an extra retry. Overwriting or deleting a result triggers recalculation of rankings and the combined leaderboard; a deleted result is pending until resolved. Overwriting is equivalent to replacing the old result with a new one in a single step. Closing a stage prevents further edits to it and activates the next stage with the advancing participants once advancement is resolved. Results cannot be corrected while a stage is closed. An administrator can reopen it only when no results exist in the subsequent stage. If results have already been entered there, they must first be deleted. The precise status terminology and ordering among forfeited participants remain to be clarified. Each game has a configured result type (at least score or time), whether a higher or lower value is better, and a configurable number of normal attempts per player. Each game is configured for one or two simultaneous players. In a two-player game, select one or two active participants and enter a result for each selected person. Any two participants with attempts remaining may be paired, even if they are on different attempt numbers. If only one person needs a result, an unofficial second player may play without being selected or recorded. Players can enter results for those attempts; an extra attempt requires an administrator to delete an existing result first. The best attempt determines the ranking for that player in that game. Each game produces its own ranking. Ranking positions award points from a configurable schedule shared by all stations, combined in the stage leaderboard; for example, 12-10-8-7-6-5-4-3-2-1 or a simpler schedule for fewer players. Positions beyond the schedule receive zero points. Players tied in a game receive the same points for their shared position, and subsequent positions are skipped accordingly (for example, a tie for second is followed by fourth). The combined leaderboard shows clearly marked preliminary standings while results are pending, recalculating as results arrive. Equal combined points share a rank, with subsequent ranks skipped. A tie-breaker is needed only when a combined tie crosses the advancement cutoff. Details of forfeits, game pools, selection, ties, and missing results remain to be decided.

The supported stage formats include a scoring round with configurable stations, multiplayer elimination, a fighting duel with multiple rounds, and a tie-breaker. A final is the role of the last stage, rather than a format restricted to a particular game type.

When an elimination stage is first, all registered participants enter automatically. The entry list is fixed when the first elimination is recorded. In a multiplayer elimination stage, participants play one game with no multiple attempts. Their elimination order determines the final ranking; the last surviving player need not be entered as eliminated.

A final can use any stage format that determines a clear winner, such as survival in direct duels or the highest score in a round. For the first tournament, the final is a fighting duel with several rounds; round wins are recorded and counted as they happen. It should support a single match or a best-of series, including best of three and best of five. A tie-breaker is one game and one match. A two-player duel can be resolved by entering its winner; for more than two tied players, the organizer chooses a game with a clear ranking and enters their order. The organizer selects suitable games for the semifinal, final, and tie-breaker directly in advance for this tournament; wheels are not required for them.
An administrator can edit results of any open stage, including correcting the elimination order. Closed stages must be reopened under the applicable reopening rule before results can be edited.

The organizer sets a shared administrator PIN when creating a tournament and can enter it on any device used for setup or protected actions such as editing or deleting entered results. Administrator access stays active on that device until logout or the browser session ends. Incomplete setup can be saved and revisited in any order, including after a crash. Essential missing settings block the start; potential issues produce warnings. Game result settings are independent per station, even if a game appears in both pools.

## Future ideas

Player profiles with pixel-art portraits and tournament statistics are ideas for later, not current requirements.

## Questions to work through

Discuss participant details and changes during a tournament, then the structure and rules of stages, station activities, results, rankings, advancement, and game selection one decision at a time.

## Agreed direction: flexible stations and game selection

A scoring stage can have any number of stations. Each participant normally plays every station, and the points from all stations are added to the combined leaderboard. Each station or other stage can use a directly chosen game or a game pool; each pool has its own wheel for random selection. When the same game is selected at multiple stations, organizers may accept it or spin again. Navigation selects a stage, then a station. The station count can be edited until the first random game is confirmed or the first result is recorded, whichever happens first. This lock remains if a game is later unlocked or a result deleted. A fixed game is ready as soon as it is saved; no confirmation is needed. The stage-level game pool also supports developer, platform, release year and description information. Existing tournaments and backups remain readable; legacy directly configured games remain playable without an additional confirmation.
